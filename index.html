Every task. Its whole story.

A task is often more than a one-liner. There’s a brief behind it, a plan, some research, a few notes about what happens next. In zdo, that thinking lives in Markdown documents linked right to the work.

  • One note, many places. Link a document to a project, a task, or a subtask, and reach it from each of them.
  • Write, split, or read. Plain Markdown with tables, checklists, diagrams, math, and code, rendered right next to your text.
  • [[Wiki links]] and attachments. Connect notes to each other and keep images and files with them.

Simple on purpose.

zdo does a handful of things and stays out of your way. Projects for work that needs structure. Quick lists for the little things you just want out of your head: groceries, packing, errands. Check them off, then discard the list.

Left out, deliberately:

  • Sign-ups
  • Team workspaces
  • AI features
  • Ads
  • Location reminders
  • Formatting toolbars

No new account. No login. Nothing sent to us.

There’s nothing to sign up for. Open zdo and start writing. Your workspace lives on your device. Want it on your iPhone too? Sync runs through your own private iCloud, so all you need is the iCloud account you already use on your Mac and iPhone.

  • No new accountNo zdo sign-up, email, or password. Use it on one device without any account.
  • No trackingNo analytics, no third-party SDKs, no usage telemetry, no ads.
  • No zdo serversYour workspace is never sent to us.
  • Sync uses your iCloudTo sync, sign in to iCloud with the same Apple Account on each device. Sync is optional.
  • Yours to takeExport Markdown and PDF, and keep automatic backups where you choose.
  • This site tooNo cookies, no analytics, no third-party fonts or scripts.

From A to Z. Then zdo.

I tried all kinds of to-do and note-taking apps, from A to Z. None quite brought together the things I wanted. So I built zdo: z + do. Option Z.

I wanted the structure of a real project tool, the calm of a good notebook, and a place for the groceries that doesn’t pretend they’re a project. And I wanted it to be mine: on my devices, with no account and nobody watching.
